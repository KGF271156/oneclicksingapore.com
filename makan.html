THE UNIFYING LANGUAGE

Makan, the taste of home.

Food is how Singaporeans recognise each other. Here is what to look for, and soon, where to find it in the world’s big cities.

The essentials

Dishes that mean home

Hainanese chicken rice

Poached chicken, fragrant rice cooked in the stock, chilli and ginger sauces: often called the national dish.

Laksa

Rice noodles in a spicy coconut broth. Katong laksa, with short noodles you eat with a spoon, is the Peranakan classic.

Chilli crab and black pepper crab

The big celebration dish, with fried mantou buns to mop up the sauce.

Kaya toast, soft-boiled eggs and kopi

The traditional breakfast: toast with coconut jam and butter, eggs with soy and pepper, and strong local coffee.

Char kway teow and Hokkien mee

Wok-fried noodles with that smoky "wok hei" flavour that is hard to copy abroad.

Roti prata, satay and nasi lemak

The Malay and Indian hawker classics that make Singapore’s food truly multicultural.

Hawker culture

A world heritage tradition

Singapore’s hawker culture was added to UNESCO’s list of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2020. More than a hundred hawker centres, from Maxwell and Lau Pa Sat to the humble estate food courts, bring all communities to the same tables. Several hawker stalls have won Michelin recognition, including a street food stall with a star.
