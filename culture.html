What carries abroad

Four languages, many dialects

English, Mandarin, Malay and Tamil are all official languages, with Malay as the national language. Hokkien, Cantonese, Teochew and Hainanese are still heard at home and at the market.

The CMIO mix

Chinese, Malay, Indian and other communities share the island, its HDB estates and its public holidays, each with its own festivals.

Peranakan heritage

The Straits-born Chinese and their blend of Chinese and Malay culture: kebaya, beaded slippers, nyonya cooking and colourful shophouses in Katong and Emerald Hill.

Heartland life

Most Singaporeans grew up in HDB flats: the void deck, the neighbourhood hawker centre and the school bus are shared memories.

Innovation and order

A reputation for clean streets, efficient transport and fast-moving technology: a way of doing things that Singaporeans abroad often miss.

Singlish

The language of belonging

Singlish mixes English with Malay, Hokkien, Cantonese and Tamil words, and carries a lot of feeling in very few syllables. For Singaporeans abroad, a lah or a can lah is a signal that you are among your own.

Lah

A particle that softens or stresses what you say: "Okay lah", "Don't worry lah".

Can / cannot

A full answer in one word: "Can?" "Can!"

Shiok

Hugely satisfying, like the first bite of a good laksa.

Kiasu

Afraid to lose out; the national instinct to queue early and to get the best deal.

Lobang

A lead, tip or opportunity: "I got a lobang for you."

Paiseh

Embarrassed or sorry: "Paiseh, I late."

Singapore abroad

Culture kept alive a generation on

Singapore societies, alumni chapters, Chinese, Malay and Indian cultural groups and student associations keep Singaporean identity alive in London, Sydney, Melbourne, San Francisco, Shanghai and beyond.
